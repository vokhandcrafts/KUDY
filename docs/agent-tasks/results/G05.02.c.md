exactly one question per transition into an armed mode. The composition root
(`controllers/`) and `app/` were not touched: the `Services.location` member and
the app-build wiring stay with G05.05, per the G06.09.b results and the G05.03.b
note.

## Mapping decisions (recorded once, here)

1. **The update mechanism follows the last requested scope.** `startFixes` maps
   to `watchPositionAsync` after a foreground request (no foreground-service
   notification on the city surface) and to `startLocationUpdatesAsync` after a
   background request — the phone-in-pocket pose of 09 §9. The service's
   `requestPermission` calls are what tell the adapter the session's intent;
   the port contract change stays within the brief's allowed split. A background
   start the OS refuses (the single `PermissionState` cannot distinguish a
   foreground-only grant) falls back to the foreground watch with the OS error
   surfaced — never swallowed.
2. **`setRegions` holds the window; no second OS geofencing task on Android.**
   The port's event contract delivers fixes only, every trigger decision is
   fix-driven (AR-5), and the continuous update stream is the Android delivery
   mechanism; an OS geofencing task would double-deliver at battery cost with
   no contract consumer. The ≤ 20-region window itself stays computed and
   pushed by the service (G05.02.b). An iOS adapter would register the regions
   as OS regions — out of scope here.
3. **Every fix event carries the subscription it runs under** (the
   service-minted generation); the adapter emits only while that subscription
   is current, so a late OS callback after `stopFixes` dies at the adapter as
   well as in the service's generation check.
4. **`OS_ACCURACY` (`Location.Accuracy.High`) is an implementation choice, not
   canon** — the 40 m gate and the 15 s watchdog are pipeline/service canon;
   this one is recorded here and calibrated on devices (G11.02, out of scope).
5. **The sync `permission()` starts honest at `'undetermined'`** and the real
   OS state arrives asynchronously as a permission event like any other; the
   service already treats undetermined as «waiting, not subscribed».
6. **Longitude range is not a shape check** — the pipeline does not gate it
   either; the mapping reuses `fixShapeReason` verbatim instead of inventing a
   parallel validation.

## Acceptance criteria — walked from the card

1. ✅ **The adapter implements the G05.02.b port exactly; the pure mapping is
   tested on synthetic inputs, including missing accuracy and out-of-range
   values (rejected by the mapping, not passed on).** `fix-mapping.ts` maps the
   structural slice of expo's `LocationObject` onto `FixInput` through the
   pipeline's exported `fixShapeReason`; nine tests cover the well-formed path,
   null/NaN accuracy (`missing-accuracy`), negative accuracy, both latitude
   bounds (accepted) and their violations, non-finite coordinates and
   timestamp; a rejected result carries no `fix` field at all. Tests:
   «AC1: …» (9), «AC1: …» permission mapping (1).
2. ✅ **Separate foreground/background permission requests.** Six tests:
   city-surface from idle → exactly the foreground question with the app-config
   string; active-guide from idle → background; the carry-over Start path →
   background with still exactly one `start 1`; disarmed transitions and
   repeated modes ask nothing; a denied arm refuses without asking; a foreground
   grant does not skip the background ask on the granted path. The explanation
   strings come from the real `app.json` read in the fixture.
3. ✅ **The foreground-service notification is declared, its text is in the app
   config, and prebuild produces a manifest with the service and the five
   location permissions (output recorded).** The notification text lives in
   `expo.extra.locationForegroundService` and reaches
   `startLocationUpdatesAsync` through the pure `location-config.ts` reader
   (the AC2/AC3 block of the demo). `npx expo prebuild --platform android
   --no-install` → the five permissions in `android/app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml`
   (the service declaration itself is a library-manifest entry, merged at build
   time), so the merged-manifest proof ran one step further:
   `cd android && ./gradlew :app:processDebugMainManifest` →
   `app/build/intermediates/merged_manifest/debug/processDebugMainManifest/AndroidManifest.xml`
   contains `expo.modules.location.services.LocationTaskService` with
   `android:foregroundServiceType="location"` and all five permissions
   (`ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION`, `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION`,
   `ACCESS_BACKGROUND_LOCATION`, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE`,
   `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_LOCATION`). The autolinking list printed by the same run
   includes `expo-task-manager (14.0.9)`.
4. ✅ **Gates green; the adapter is reachable only through the composition
   root.** Fresh numbers at final HEAD in «Commands and outcomes». Reachability:
   no module outside `services/location/expo/expo-location-port.ts` imports the
   adapter (import grep below); `app/` is kept out of `services/` by
